### What we can say now

1. **Search helps, but one run per arm cannot certify it.** grep beats base by 7
   solves (50 vs 43, twice — r2 says 50 too), exact McNemar p = 0.065 / 0.092.
   The noise floor says a k=1 comparison only resolves gaps ≥ ~9 solves. That is
   exactly why base r2 is running: at k=2 per side the bar drops to 6.3, so a
   +7 effect becomes resolvable — the grid's first certifiable arm effect, if it holds.
2. **Which retrieval barely matters at full budget.** grep vs semantic is 1 solve
   apart, 7 discordant, p = 1.0. Discovery-vs-confirmation retrieval is a wash at $1.
3. **Search's real value is at low budget.** At a $0.10 cap search nearly doubles
   solves (base 13 vs grep 23 / semantic 24); by $1.00 base has closed to within 7.
   Semantic front-loads (best arm at $0.25, 39 vs grep's 34), grep finishes better.
   A pass@1 headline at a generous cap reads the experiment where it has least to
   say — that is a finding about evaluation practice, and it is COMPARE.md §2's curve.
4. **The noise floor is a headline result on its own.** Two byte-identical grep runs
   flip 10 problems out of 90. Any single-run harness comparison at this scale claiming
   an effect smaller than ~9 solves is reading noise — which is most published
   architecture comparisons.
5. **Verified scratch compilation looks like the active ingredient, and search may be
   partly its proxy.** snippetonly — no retrieval at all, just compile-any-snippet —
   lands 48/90, indistinguishable from grep's 50 (p = 0.77). snippet (grep + snippet)
   is at 51 with 12 problems still out. If it lands ≥ 53, it clears even the k=1 bar
   over base. The substitution analysis (do search calls collapse when snippet is
   available?) runs once the cell closes.
6. **Hill-climb honesty.** With F = 10, arms landing within a few solves are not
   separable, so "block winner" is a *stated choice*, not a measurement — grep over
   semantic (block A), snippet assumed over snippetonly (block B). Every block-C
   number is conditional on that path and gets reported that way.
7. **The benchmark is thinning.** 36/90 problems are solved by every arm, 32 by
   none — only 22 carry information. If block C also lands inside the noise floor,
   the standing decision is to move the grid to Putnam mid-difficulty.
8. **Agents do not destroy their own solves, and solves are front-loaded.** All 213
   attempts that kept editing after reaching a verified proof got back to green;
   cost-at-first-proof is 92% of cost-at-end, so only ~8% of spend is post-solve.
9. **Money.** A cell costs $41–45 at the $1 cap ($0.82–1.02 per solve); the whole
   project ledger stands at ≈ $457 including the two cells in flight.

**AUC** is the area under the arm's solve-count-vs-budget curve from $0 to the $1
cap: every solve contributes (cap − first-proof cost), so a $0.05 solve adds ~0.95,
a solve at the cap adds ~0, and an unsolved problem adds nothing. Max 90 (every
problem solved for free). It rewards solving *cheaply*, not just solving — two arms
with equal solve counts differ in AUC if one gets its proofs earlier in the budget.
